# Purpose

The Governance Domain Assessment evaluates the organizational structures, leadership practices, policies, accountability mechanisms, and decision-making processes that establish and sustain Magnolia Regional Medical Center's cybersecurity program.

Effective governance provides executive oversight, defines accountability, aligns cybersecurity with organizational objectives, supports regulatory compliance, and enables informed risk management.

This assessment determines whether governance activities are appropriately designed, implemented, monitored, and continuously improved.

---

# Domain Objectives

The Governance Domain Assessment is designed to determine whether:

- Executive leadership provides cybersecurity oversight.
- Cybersecurity responsibilities are clearly assigned.
- Policies and standards are formally approved.
- Enterprise risks are governed effectively.
- Regulatory obligations are managed.
- Cybersecurity receives appropriate funding.
- Governance committees operate effectively.
- Risk decisions are documented.
- Security strategy aligns with business objectives.
- Continuous improvement processes exist.

# Consultant Notes

## Best Practices

- Begin with executive interviews before detailed control testing.
- Review governance documentation before interviewing stakeholders.
- Validate that governance practices match documented policies.
- Focus on executive accountability and decision-making.
- Evaluate governance effectiveness, not just document existence.

## Common Pitfalls

- Assuming policy approval equals governance effectiveness.
- Overlooking informal governance practices.
- Failing to review board reporting.
- Ignoring budget and resource governance.
- Treating governance as purely administrative.

## Red Flags

- No formally appointed CISO.
- Cybersecurity absent from executive agendas.
- Policies lack ownership or approval.
- Enterprise risk register excludes cyber risks.
- Security metrics are not reported to leadership.
- Governance committees meet infrequently or not at all.
- Executive leadership cannot articulate cybersecurity objectives.
