# Purpose

This workpaper documents testing performed to determine whether Magnolia Regional Medical Center has implemented an effective cybersecurity metrics and executive reporting program that provides leadership with timely, accurate, risk-based information for governance and decision-making.

---

# Control Objective

Determine whether executive leadership receives meaningful cybersecurity metrics that support oversight, strategic planning, regulatory compliance, and enterprise risk management.

# Risk Statement

Without meaningful cybersecurity metrics and executive reporting, leadership may be unable to effectively evaluate organizational risk, prioritize investments, measure program performance, or make informed governance decisions regarding the protection of ePHI.

---

# Control Description

The organization should provide executive leadership with cybersecurity reporting that includes:

- Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)
- Key Risk Indicators (KRIs)
- Security trends
- Compliance status
- Risk posture
- Incident reporting
- Vulnerability trends
- Third-party risk metrics
- Strategic initiative progress
- Regulatory reporting

---

# Testing Procedures

The following procedures were performed:

1. Obtained executive cybersecurity dashboards.
2. Reviewed quarterly board presentations.
3. Evaluated KPI definitions.
4. Reviewed reporting frequency.
5. Compared reported metrics to governance objectives.
6. Interviewed executive stakeholders.
7. Verified supporting evidence.
8. Evaluated strategic reporting maturity.
9. Reviewed dashboard trend analysis.
10. Compared reporting against industry best practices.

# Results

Testing confirmed that executive leadership receives recurring cybersecurity dashboards and quarterly board presentations.

Reports consistently include:

- Security incidents
- Vulnerability counts
- Patch compliance
- Security awareness completion
- Open audit findings
- HIPAA compliance updates
- Strategic initiative status

However, reporting is primarily operational in nature.

Executive dashboards provide limited insight into long-term enterprise cyber risk, residual risk trends, business impact analysis, and predictive risk indicators.

---

✓ Monthly executive reporting established.

## Improvement Opportunities

Strategic governance reporting can be enhanced by incorporating:

- Enterprise Key Risk Indicators (KRIs)
- Residual risk trends
- Risk appetite metrics
- Business impact analysis
- Industry benchmarking
- Cybersecurity maturity trends
- Third-party risk exposure
- Financial risk exposure
- Control effectiveness trends

---

# Exceptions Identified

### Exception GOV-006-01

Executive cybersecurity reporting emphasizes operational performance metrics but provides limited strategic risk analytics needed to support long-term governance decisions.

---

# Root Cause Analysis

The cybersecurity reporting program evolved from operational IT reporting.

As executive governance responsibilities expanded, dashboards were not redesigned to include strategic governance metrics expected by senior leadership and governing boards.

---

# Business Impact

Without strategic reporting:

- Enterprise cyber risk may be underestimated.
- Executive prioritization becomes more difficult.
- Investment decisions rely primarily on operational information.
- Long-term program effectiveness is harder to measure.
- Board oversight may be less effective.

No immediate operational deficiencies were identified.

### Justification

Executive reporting is formalized, consistently delivered, and supports operational governance.

However, reporting lacks advanced risk analytics, predictive metrics, and strategic performance measurements required to achieve Managed & Measured maturity.

---

# Control Effectiveness

**Partially Effective**

The reporting process functions reliably but does not fully support executive strategic decision-making.

---

# Residual Risk

**Moderate**

Operational oversight is effective; however, limited strategic reporting increases the likelihood that emerging enterprise cybersecurity risks may not be identified or prioritized as effectively as possible.

---

**Moderate**

---

# Recommendations

The organization should enhance executive cybersecurity reporting by:

- Implementing enterprise Key Risk Indicators (KRIs).
- Reporting residual risk by business function.
- Establishing cybersecurity risk appetite thresholds.
- Including trend analysis for major risk categories.
- Reporting cybersecurity maturity scores.
- Incorporating peer healthcare benchmarking.
- Providing predictive cyber risk indicators.
- Linking security investments to measurable business outcomes.

---

# Workpaper Conclusion

Magnolia Regional Medical Center has implemented a reliable executive cybersecurity reporting process that supports operational governance.

The assessment identified an opportunity to strengthen executive decision-making by expanding reporting beyond operational KPIs to include strategic risk analytics and governance-focused metrics.

Addressing this recommendation will improve executive oversight, board reporting, and long-term cybersecurity program maturity.
