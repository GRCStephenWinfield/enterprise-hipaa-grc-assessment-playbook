# Purpose

This workpaper documents testing performed to determine whether Magnolia Regional Medical Center maintains an effective cybersecurity budgeting and investment governance process that aligns financial resources with enterprise risk, patient safety, regulatory compliance, and strategic cybersecurity objectives.

---

# Control Objective

Determine whether cybersecurity funding decisions are governed through a formal process that aligns investments with organizational priorities, enterprise risks, regulatory obligations, and long-term cybersecurity strategy.

# Risk Statement

Without effective financial governance, cybersecurity investments may not adequately address enterprise risks, resulting in underfunded security initiatives, delayed remediation activities, regulatory exposure, and increased risk to ePHI.

---

# Control Description

The organization should maintain a cybersecurity budgeting process that:

- Aligns funding with enterprise risk.
- Supports strategic initiatives.
- Prioritizes remediation activities.
- Includes executive approval.
- Supports regulatory compliance.
- Is reviewed annually.
- Measures investment effectiveness.
- Supports continuous improvement.

---

# Testing Procedures

The following procedures were performed:

1. Reviewed annual cybersecurity budget documentation.
2. Verified executive approval.
3. Compared budget allocations to strategic initiatives.
4. Evaluated funding for remediation activities.
5. Reviewed capital investment requests.
6. Reviewed budget variance reports.
7. Compared investments against enterprise risks.
8. Interviewed executive leadership.
9. Evaluated funding governance process.
10. Assessed budgeting maturity.

# Results

Testing confirmed that cybersecurity funding is incorporated into the organization's annual budgeting process and receives executive review and approval.

Evidence demonstrated:

- Budget requests are linked to strategic initiatives.
- Funding decisions consider enterprise cyber risk.
- Executive leadership reviews major investments.
- Capital requests include business justification.
- Remediation projects receive prioritized funding.
- Budget performance is monitored throughout the fiscal year.

Testing also identified that while annual planning is mature, formal multi-year investment planning is limited.

---

# Assessor Observations

## Positive Observations

✓ Executive oversight of cybersecurity spending.

✓ Funding aligned with enterprise strategy.

✓ Major initiatives supported by business cases.

✓ Budget requests incorporate risk considerations.

✓ Quarterly budget reviews documented.

✓ Strong collaboration between Finance and Information Security.

---

## Improvement Opportunities

Develop a rolling three-to-five-year cybersecurity investment roadmap that:

- Forecasts technology lifecycle replacement.
- Anticipates regulatory requirements.
- Projects staffing requirements.
- Supports long-term resilience planning.
- Estimates future operational costs.

---

# Exceptions Identified

No material deficiencies identified.

One strategic planning enhancement documented.

---

# Root Cause Analysis

Budget governance is centered on the annual planning cycle.

Long-range investment forecasting has not been formalized because organizational budgeting primarily focuses on yearly operational planning.

---

# Business Impact

Current budgeting practices effectively support operational cybersecurity requirements.

Expanding long-term financial planning would:

- Improve strategic investment decisions.
- Reduce unexpected capital expenditures.
- Better anticipate evolving cyber threats.
- Support sustainable program growth.

# Recommendations

To further mature budget governance:

- Develop a rolling three-to-five-year cybersecurity investment roadmap.
- Forecast technology refresh cycles.
- Integrate cyber insurance trends into planning.
- Include staffing forecasts.
- Measure return on security investments (ROSI).
- Benchmark cybersecurity spending against comparable healthcare organizations.

---

# Workpaper Conclusion

Magnolia Regional Medical Center maintains a mature cybersecurity budget governance process that aligns financial resources with strategic objectives and enterprise risk.

The assessment found no material deficiencies. The addition of long-term investment planning would further improve financial governance and organizational resilience.
