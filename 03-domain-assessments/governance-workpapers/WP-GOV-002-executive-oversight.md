# Purpose

This workpaper documents testing performed to determine whether executive leadership provides effective governance and oversight of Magnolia Regional Medical Center's cybersecurity program through established governance structures, recurring executive reviews, strategic decision-making, and accountability mechanisms.

---

# Control Objective

Determine whether executive leadership actively governs cybersecurity through formal oversight, strategic direction, performance monitoring, and risk-based decision-making.

# Risk Statement

Without active executive oversight, cybersecurity risks may not receive appropriate organizational attention, resulting in delayed decision-making, ineffective governance, inadequate resource allocation, and increased risk to systems containing electronic protected health information (ePHI).

---

# Control Description

Executive leadership should:

- Review cybersecurity performance regularly.
- Receive cybersecurity dashboards.
- Monitor enterprise cyber risks.
- Review major incidents.
- Approve strategic initiatives.
- Allocate cybersecurity funding.
- Track remediation progress.
- Establish organizational risk tolerance.

---

# Testing Procedures

The following procedures were performed:

1. Reviewed Executive Cybersecurity Steering Committee Charter.
2. Reviewed Governance Committee meeting minutes for the previous twelve months.
3. Examined Board cybersecurity presentation materials.
4. Verified quarterly executive cybersecurity reporting.
5. Confirmed executive participation in governance meetings.
6. Reviewed strategic cybersecurity initiatives presented to executive leadership.
7. Compared governance meeting discussions with Enterprise Risk Register entries.
8. Validated interview responses with documentary evidence.
9. Reviewed follow-up actions from prior governance meetings.
10. Evaluated executive oversight against framework requirements.

# Results

Executive leadership demonstrated active participation in cybersecurity governance.

Evidence confirmed that:

- Quarterly Governance Committee meetings occurred as scheduled.
- Executive dashboards were presented consistently.
- Cybersecurity was discussed as an enterprise business risk.
- Strategic initiatives received executive review.
- Budget requests were reviewed through executive governance.
- Enterprise cyber risks were incorporated into organizational risk discussions.

Review of meeting minutes demonstrated active participation from executive leadership, including requests for additional metrics, remediation updates, and risk trend analysis.

---

# Assessor Observations

## Positive Observations

✓ Executive Sponsor consistently attended governance meetings.

✓ Cybersecurity received dedicated agenda time.

✓ Enterprise risks were reviewed quarterly.

✓ Budget discussions aligned with strategic priorities.

✓ Executive leadership demonstrated understanding of cyber risk.

✓ Governance committee maintained documented action items.

---

## Improvement Opportunities

The governance dashboard focused primarily on operational security metrics.

Limited reporting was available regarding:

- Cyber risk trends over time.
- Business impact of unresolved risks.
- Risk tolerance metrics.
- Benchmarking against peer healthcare organizations.

---

# Exceptions Identified

No material control deficiencies identified.

One governance maturity enhancement opportunity documented.

---

# Control Effectiveness

**Effective**

---

# Residual Risk

**Low**

Executive oversight significantly reduces governance risk; however, additional strategic metrics would further strengthen executive decision-making.

---

# Potential Finding

None.

This observation will be tracked as a governance maturity recommendation rather than a formal finding.

---

# Recommendations

Enhance executive reporting by incorporating:

- Enterprise cyber risk trend analysis.
- Residual risk dashboards.
- Key Risk Indicators (KRIs).
- Security maturity trends.
- Regulatory compliance scorecards.
- Industry benchmarking.
- Strategic initiative progress metrics.

---

# Workpaper Conclusion

Executive leadership provides active and effective oversight of Magnolia Regional Medical Center's cybersecurity program.

Governance structures are established, executive engagement is evident, and cybersecurity is managed as an enterprise risk.

While opportunities exist to enhance strategic reporting, the control is appropriately designed and operating effectively.
