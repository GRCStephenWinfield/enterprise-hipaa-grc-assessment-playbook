# Purpose

This workpaper documents testing performed to determine whether Magnolia Regional Medical Center's Cybersecurity Governance Committee operates effectively, provides executive oversight, documents decisions, monitors strategic initiatives, and maintains accountability for cybersecurity governance.

---

# Control Objective

Determine whether the Governance Committee operates according to its charter and provides effective oversight of cybersecurity strategy, enterprise risk, regulatory compliance, and security program performance.

# Risk Statement

Without an effective governance committee, executive leadership may not adequately oversee cybersecurity strategy, enterprise risk, compliance obligations, or major security initiatives, resulting in ineffective governance and delayed decision-making.

---

# Control Description

The Governance Committee should:

- Meet on a scheduled basis.
- Include appropriate executive representation.
- Review enterprise cyber risks.
- Monitor strategic initiatives.
- Review significant security incidents.
- Track regulatory compliance.
- Review audit findings.
- Monitor remediation activities.
- Document decisions and action items.
- Escalate significant issues to executive leadership.

---

# Testing Procedures

The following procedures were performed:

1. Reviewed Governance Committee Charter.
2. Examined meeting schedules.
3. Reviewed attendance records.
4. Evaluated meeting agendas.
5. Reviewed committee minutes.
6. Verified action-item tracking.
7. Reviewed escalation procedures.
8. Evaluated executive participation.
9. Compared meeting discussions with Risk Register updates.
10. Assessed governance maturity.

# Results

Testing confirmed that the Governance Committee operates according to its approved charter.

Evidence demonstrated:

- Meetings occur monthly.
- Executive attendance consistently exceeds 90%.
- Cybersecurity strategy is reviewed regularly.
- Enterprise cyber risks are discussed.
- Regulatory compliance is monitored.
- Security incidents receive executive attention.
- Strategic initiatives are tracked.

However, review of action-item documentation identified inconsistencies in documenting completion evidence for several assigned actions.

While action items were generally completed, supporting evidence of completion was not consistently retained within committee records.

---

# Assessor Observations

## Positive Observations

✓ Committee meets consistently.

✓ Executive participation is strong.

✓ Governance discussions are substantive.

✓ Strategic initiatives receive executive oversight.

✓ Enterprise risks are reviewed regularly.

✓ Meeting minutes accurately reflect discussions.

---

## Improvement Opportunities

Committee action tracking should include:

- Completion evidence.
- Validation dates.
- Independent verification.
- Formal closure approval.
- Outstanding action aging.

---

# Exceptions Identified

## Exception GOV-009-01

Eight of twenty-five sampled committee action items lacked documented evidence demonstrating formal completion or management validation.

---

# Root Cause Analysis

The committee maintains an action-item register but has not standardized documentation requirements for demonstrating completion.

Committee administrators rely on verbal status updates rather than retaining objective supporting evidence.

---

# Business Impact

Weak documentation of committee action-item closure may:

- Reduce executive accountability.
- Delay remediation validation.
- Limit audit traceability.
- Increase the likelihood of recurring governance issues.

No evidence indicated that critical actions remained unresolved.

**Title**

Governance Committee Action Items Are Not Consistently Validated Before Closure

**Severity**

**Moderate**

---

# Risk Reference

## RSK-GOV-004

Committee governance may be weakened when corrective actions are closed without documented evidence demonstrating successful implementation.

---

# Recommendations

The organization should:

1. Require documented evidence for every completed action item.
2. Assign formal validation responsibilities.
3. Record validation dates.
4. Require committee approval before closure.
5. Implement action-item aging reports.
6. Include overdue actions within executive dashboards.
7. Perform quarterly QA reviews of committee documentation.

---

# Workpaper Conclusion

Magnolia Regional Medical Center's Governance Committee provides effective executive oversight and functions as an important component of the organization's cybersecurity governance program.

The assessment identified an opportunity to strengthen governance documentation by improving validation and evidence retention for committee action items.

This weakness does not significantly impair governance effectiveness but should be addressed to improve accountability, audit readiness, and continuous improvement.
