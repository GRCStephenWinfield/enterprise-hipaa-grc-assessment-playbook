# Purpose

This workpaper documents testing performed to determine whether Magnolia Regional Medical Center has established, documented, communicated, and maintained clearly defined cybersecurity roles and responsibilities across executive leadership, Information Technology, Information Security, Privacy, Compliance, Internal Audit, Human Resources, and business units.

---

# Control Objective

Determine whether cybersecurity responsibilities are appropriately assigned, documented, communicated, understood, and periodically reviewed to support the organization's governance framework and regulatory obligations.

# Risk Statement

If cybersecurity roles and responsibilities are not clearly defined and consistently communicated, critical security activities may not be performed, accountability may be weakened, and regulatory obligations related to the protection of ePHI may not be fulfilled.

---

# Control Description

The organization should maintain documented cybersecurity responsibilities that define accountability for:

- Executive leadership
- Chief Information Security Officer
- Chief Information Officer
- Privacy Officer
- Compliance Officer
- Internal Audit
- Human Resources
- Department Managers
- System Owners
- Data Owners
- End Users
- Third-Party Providers

---

# Testing Procedures

The following procedures were performed:

1. Reviewed the Security Roles and Responsibilities Matrix.
2. Compared responsibilities against the organizational chart.
3. Reviewed applicable job descriptions.
4. Examined committee charters.
5. Verified executive accountability.
6. Reviewed policy ownership assignments.
7. Interviewed key governance stakeholders.
8. Evaluated segregation of duties.
9. Compared documented responsibilities with operational practices.
10. Assessed overall governance maturity.

# Results

Testing determined that cybersecurity responsibilities have been formally documented across executive leadership and key governance functions.

Evidence confirmed:

- Executive accountability is established.
- Security ownership is documented.
- Privacy and Compliance responsibilities are defined.
- Governance committees have assigned responsibilities.
- Segregation of duties is generally appropriate.
- Job descriptions align with organizational responsibilities.

However, assessment procedures identified inconsistencies in documenting cybersecurity responsibilities for several business-unit system owners.

Although these responsibilities are performed operationally, they are not consistently documented within formal role descriptions.

---

# Assessor Observations

## Positive Observations

✓ Clearly defined executive accountability.

✓ Dedicated CISO reporting structure.

✓ Effective coordination between Security, Privacy, Compliance, and Internal Audit.

✓ Governance committee responsibilities are well documented.

✓ Strong segregation of duties within Information Security.

---

## Improvement Opportunities

Business-unit ownership documentation should be standardized across all departments.

Several application owners described cybersecurity responsibilities during interviews that were not reflected within formal documentation.

---

# Exceptions Identified

### Exception GOV-004-01

Formal cybersecurity responsibilities for selected business-unit application owners were not consistently documented within role descriptions.

Operational ownership exists; however, documentation is incomplete.

---

# Root Cause Analysis

The organization has historically focused role documentation on Information Technology and Information Security personnel.

Business-unit ownership responsibilities have evolved over time without a corresponding update to Human Resources role descriptions.

---

# Business Impact

Incomplete role documentation may result in:

- Inconsistent accountability
- Delayed risk ownership
- Reduced effectiveness during personnel transitions
- Audit observations regarding governance documentation

Current operational impact is considered limited because interview responses demonstrated that responsibilities are generally understood.

**Moderate**

# Finding Reference

**FIN-GOV-001**

**Title: Business-Unit Cybersecurity Responsibilities Are Not Consistently Documented**

Severity:

**Moderate**

---

# Recommendations

The organization should:

- Update all business-unit system owner role descriptions.
- Standardize cybersecurity responsibility statements across departments.
- Require annual review of security-related job responsibilities.
- Incorporate responsibility validation into annual governance reviews.
- Coordinate updates between Human Resources and Information Security.

---

# Workpaper Conclusion

Magnolia Regional Medical Center has implemented a mature governance structure with clearly defined cybersecurity responsibilities across executive leadership and Information Security functions.

The assessment identified an opportunity to strengthen governance by formalizing cybersecurity responsibilities for business-unit system owners.

This observation does not represent a systemic governance failure but should be addressed to improve accountability and audit readiness.
