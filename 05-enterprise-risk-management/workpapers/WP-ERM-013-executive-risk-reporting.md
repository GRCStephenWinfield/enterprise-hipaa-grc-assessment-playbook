# Purpose

This workpaper evaluates whether Magnolia Regional Medical Center provides executive leadership with timely, accurate, meaningful, and actionable cybersecurity risk reporting that supports strategic decision-making.

The assessment determines whether executive reporting accurately reflects the organization's cybersecurity risk posture, aligns with business objectives, and enables informed governance decisions.

---

# Control Objective

Determine whether executive cybersecurity reporting:

- Accurately reflects enterprise cyber risk.
- Supports executive decision-making.
- Includes strategic Key Risk Indicators (KRIs).
- Includes meaningful trends.
- Highlights significant emerging risks.
- Supports investment prioritization.
- Communicates regulatory exposure.
- Includes remediation status and residual risk.

# Risks Addressed

Failure to provide effective executive cybersecurity reporting may result in:

- Poor strategic decisions.
- Delayed risk treatment.
- Inadequate cybersecurity investment.
- Ineffective governance.
- Regulatory deficiencies.
- Misunderstanding of enterprise risk.
- Failure to address emerging threats.
- Increased organizational exposure.

# Evaluation Criteria

### Effective

- Executive reporting is timely.
- Reports are accurate.
- Strategic metrics are included.
- KRIs are meaningful.
- Recommendations support decisions.
- Reporting aligns with enterprise objectives.

### Partially Effective

- Reports are generally effective.
- Limited strategic metrics.
- Minor inconsistencies.
- Recommendations lack sufficient detail.

### Ineffective

- Reporting is operational only.
- Strategic information absent.
- Inaccurate or incomplete reporting.
- Executives lack actionable information.
