# Purpose

This workpaper evaluates whether Magnolia Regional Medical Center has implemented a formal and repeatable process for identifying, validating, documenting, prioritizing, and communicating cybersecurity threats that could affect organizational operations, patient safety, electronic protected health information (ePHI), and regulatory compliance.

The assessment determines whether threat intelligence is integrated into enterprise risk management and influences organizational risk decisions.

---

# Control Objective

Determine whether the organization:

- Identifies internal and external cyber threats.
- Maintains formal threat intelligence processes.
- Incorporates healthcare-specific threats.
- Evaluates emerging threats.
- Updates enterprise risks based on threat intelligence.
- Communicates significant threats to leadership.
- Supports proactive cybersecurity planning.

# Risks Addressed

Failure to identify relevant cyber threats may result in:

- Emerging attacks remaining undetected.
- Inadequate enterprise risk assessments.
- Delayed response to ransomware.
- Increased patient-safety exposure.
- Regulatory deficiencies.
- Poor investment decisions.
- Ineffective security monitoring.

# Evaluation Criteria

### Effective

- Formal threat intelligence process
- Multiple intelligence sources
- Healthcare-specific intelligence
- Executive communication
- Enterprise risk integration
- Continuous monitoring

### Partially Effective

- Threat intelligence exists
- Limited sources
- Irregular reporting
- Minor integration gaps

### Ineffective

- No formal process
- Reactive only
- Limited intelligence
- No executive reporting
- Threats not incorporated into risk management
