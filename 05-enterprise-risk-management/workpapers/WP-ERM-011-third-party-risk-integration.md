# Purpose

This workpaper evaluates whether Magnolia Regional Medical Center integrates third-party cybersecurity risks into its Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) program.

The assessment determines whether cybersecurity risks associated with vendors, Business Associates, cloud providers, managed service providers, medical device manufacturers, software suppliers, and other external organizations are identified, assessed, monitored, and reported as part of enterprise risk management.

---

# Control Objective

Determine whether the organization:

- Identifies critical third parties.
- Performs cybersecurity risk assessments before onboarding.
- Performs ongoing third-party risk monitoring.
- Integrates third-party risks into the Enterprise Risk Register.
- Evaluates Business Associate cybersecurity risks.
- Evaluates cloud-service risks.
- Evaluates software supply-chain risks.
- Reports significant third-party risks to executive leadership.

# Risks Addressed

Failure to integrate third-party cybersecurity risks into enterprise risk management may result in:

- Supply-chain compromise.
- Vendor-related ransomware incidents.
- Business Associate HIPAA violations.
- Cloud-service outages.
- Medical device compromise.
- Third-party access abuse.
- Regulatory enforcement.
- Patient-care disruption.
- Financial loss.
- Reputational damage.

# Evaluation Criteria

### Effective

- Mature TPRM program
- Current vendor inventory
- Risk-based assessments
- Continuous monitoring
- ERM integration
- Executive reporting

### Partially Effective

- TPRM program exists
- Minor documentation gaps
- Limited monitoring
- Partial ERM integration

### Ineffective

- No formal TPRM program
- No vendor assessments
- Incomplete inventories
- No executive oversight
- No ERM integration
