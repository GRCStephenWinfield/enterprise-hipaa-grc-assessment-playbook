# Purpose

This document establishes the planned timeline, sequencing, milestones, dependencies, review periods, and reporting cadence for the Enterprise HIPAA Cybersecurity Assessment.

The schedule converts the Master Assessment Plan into a time-bound execution roadmap that enables the assessment team and Magnolia Regional Medical Center to coordinate evidence collection, interviews, fieldwork, quality reviews, management validation, remediation planning, and executive reporting.

# Scheduling Principles

The assessment schedule follows these principles:

- Patient care and clinical operations take priority.
- High-risk domains are assessed early enough to allow escalation.
- Evidence requests are issued before related interviews and testing.
- Shared dependencies are reviewed before dependent domains.
- Quality reviews occur throughout the engagement rather than only at the end.
- Management receives sufficient time to validate preliminary findings.
- Material schedule changes follow formal change control.
- Critical risks are escalated immediately and are not delayed until routine reporting.

---

# Schedule Assumptions

The schedule assumes:

- Executive sponsorship remains active.
- Required stakeholders are available within agreed timeframes.
- Initial evidence is provided within ten business days of request.
- Follow-up evidence is provided within five business days.
- Interviews can generally be completed within sixty to ninety minutes.
- Domain assessors can work concurrently where dependencies permit.
- Clinical operations will not be disrupted.
- Major scope changes will be formally approved.
- No major incident materially interrupts the engagement.

---

# Schedule Constraints

The schedule must account for:

- Clinical staffing requirements
- Patient-care priorities
- Production change freezes
- Regulatory deadlines
- Vendor maintenance windows
- Executive availability
- Limited testing windows
- Restricted access to live PHI and ePHI
- Legacy-system documentation limitations
- Quality-review and management-validation periods

# Schedule Variance Thresholds

| Variance | Required Action |
|----------|-----------------|
| 0–2 Business Days | Manage within the assessment team |
| 3–5 Business Days | Document mitigation and notify Client Coordinator |
| 6–10 Business Days | Escalate to CISO and Executive Sponsor |
| More Than 10 Business Days | Initiate formal schedule change request |

Any delay affecting final deliverables, critical testing, or executive reporting requires formal review regardless of duration.

---

# Schedule Change Control

A schedule change request is required when:

- A major milestone changes.
- Domain fieldwork moves by more than five business days.
- Evidence delays affect assessment conclusions.
- Scope changes create new activities.
- Critical stakeholders are unavailable.
- A material incident interrupts the engagement.
- Quality-review requirements cannot be met.

Each schedule change request should document:

- Requested change
- Reason
- Affected milestones
- Scope impact
- Resource impact
- Risk impact
- Deliverable impact
- Approval decision

# Consultant Notes

## Best Practices

- Build the schedule around dependencies rather than simply assigning dates.
- Issue evidence requests before interviews whenever practical.
- Reserve time for management validation and quality review.
- Track the critical path separately from ordinary activities.
- Update status regularly without silently changing the approved baseline.

## Common Pitfalls

- Scheduling every domain sequentially when safe parallel work is possible.
- Underestimating evidence and stakeholder delays.
- Leaving no time for report review and revisions.
- Treating the final readout as the final drafting deadline.
- Ignoring clinical calendars and operational restrictions.

## Red Flags

- The schedule cannot support the approved scope.
- Most evidence is due after interviews begin.
- No time is reserved for quality assurance.
- Several critical stakeholders are scheduled concurrently.
- Domain dependencies are ignored.
- Final reporting depends on unvalidated findings.
- Milestones lack accountable owners.
