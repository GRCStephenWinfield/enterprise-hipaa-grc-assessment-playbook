# Purpose

This document establishes the formal process for identifying, documenting, evaluating, escalating, resolving, and closing issues encountered during the Enterprise HIPAA Cybersecurity Assessment.

The procedure ensures that assessment risks, blockers, client concerns, evidence delays, technical problems, and significant security issues are addressed consistently, transparently, and in a timely manner.

An issue is any event or condition that threatens the successful completion of the engagement, affects assessment quality, introduces unacceptable risk, or requires management attention.

# Objectives

This procedure is designed to:

- Ensure timely issue identification.
- Prevent assessment delays.
- Protect assessment quality.
- Establish clear ownership.
- Define escalation authority.
- Document resolution decisions.
- Support executive awareness.
- Maintain engagement momentum.

---

# Guiding Principles

Every issue shall be:

- Logged immediately.
- Assigned an owner.
- Given a priority.
- Reviewed objectively.
- Escalated when necessary.
- Tracked until closure.
- Supported by evidence.
- Communicated appropriately.

# Immediate Escalation Events

The following events require immediate notification:

- Confirmed ransomware
- Active compromise of ePHI
- Patient safety impacts
- Regulatory notification event
- Evidence of fraud
- Insider threat
- Unauthorized privileged access
- Material legal issue
- Executive-requested escalation
- Media attention

These events bypass normal escalation timelines.

# Consultant Notes

## Best Practices

- Escalate early rather than late.
- Focus on facts.
- Keep stakeholders informed.
- Track every issue to closure.
- Separate symptoms from root causes.
- Update the Issue Register immediately after significant events.

## Common Pitfalls

- Waiting too long to escalate.
- Closing issues without validation.
- Assigning multiple owners.
- Missing executive communication.
- Poor documentation.

## Red Flags

- Critical issues remain open without executive awareness.
- Repeated schedule delays.
- Missing evidence impacting findings.
- Multiple unresolved scope disputes.
- High-severity issues without owners.
- Repeated vendor failures.
