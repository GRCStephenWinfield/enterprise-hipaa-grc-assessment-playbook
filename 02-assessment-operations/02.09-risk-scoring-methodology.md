# Purpose

This document establishes the standardized methodology used to identify, analyze, score, prioritize, communicate, and monitor cybersecurity risks identified during the Enterprise HIPAA Cybersecurity Assessment.

The methodology ensures that risk ratings are:

- Consistent
- Defensible
- Repeatable
- Evidence-based
- Business-focused
- Patient-safety focused
- Traceable to assessment findings

# Objectives

The methodology is designed to:

- Evaluate cybersecurity risk consistently.
- Prioritize remediation activities.
- Support executive decision-making.
- Measure residual risk after controls.
- Support HIPAA Security Rule Risk Analysis.
- Align findings to enterprise risk management.
- Support Board reporting.

---

# Risk Formula

```text
Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact
```

Example:

```text
Likelihood = 4

Impact = 5

Overall Risk = 20 (Critical)
```

---

# Likelihood Scale

| Score | Definition |
|------:|------------|
| 1 | Rare |
| 2 | Unlikely |
| 3 | Possible |
| 4 | Likely |
| 5 | Almost Certain |

# Inherent vs Residual Risk

## Inherent Risk

Risk before any controls exist.

Example:

```text
Internet-facing patient portal

Likelihood = 5

Impact = 5

Inherent Risk = 25
```

---

## Residual Risk

Risk remaining after controls operate.

Example:

```text
MFA

Web Application Firewall

Continuous Monitoring

Residual Risk = 10
```

# Risk Statement Format

Each risk should be written as:

```text
Because <condition>,

there is a possibility that <event>

which could result in <business impact>.
```

Example

```text
Because privileged accounts are not protected with MFA,

there is a possibility of credential compromise,

which could result in unauthorized access to ePHI.
```

# Risk Treatment Options

| Option | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| Mitigate | Reduce risk with controls |
| Accept | Formally accept remaining risk |
| Transfer | Shift financial impact (insurance, contracts) |
| Avoid | Eliminate activity creating the risk |

---

# Risk Escalation

Immediate escalation is required for:

- Critical HIPAA violations
- Confirmed compromise of ePHI
- Unsupported critical medical devices
- Internet-facing systems without MFA
- Active ransomware
- Patient safety risks
- Missing backups for critical systems
- Regulatory notification events

---

# Risk Acceptance

High or Critical risks shall not be accepted without documented approval.

Minimum documentation:

- Business justification
- Residual risk
- Compensating controls
- Expiration date
- Executive approval

# Consultant Notes

## Best Practices

- Score business impact—not technical severity alone.
- Patient safety should outweigh technical inconvenience.
- Re-score risks after remediation.
- Link every risk to a supporting finding.

## Common Pitfalls

- Inflating every risk to Critical.
- Ignoring compensating controls.
- Confusing vulnerabilities with risks.
- Leaving risks without owners.

## Red Flags

- Multiple Critical risks without executive awareness.
- Accepted High risks without documentation.
- Risks older than one year.
- Repeat findings across assessments.
