# Purpose

This document establishes the methodology used to identify, evaluate, document, validate, classify, communicate, and track assessment findings throughout the Enterprise HIPAA Cybersecurity Assessment.

A finding represents a supported conclusion that a cybersecurity control, process, or governance activity does not fully satisfy an applicable requirement or adequately manage identified risk.

All findings shall be:

- Evidence-based
- Objective
- Reproducible
- Traceable
- Risk-focused
- Actionable
- Independently reviewed
- Written in language appropriate for executive leadership

Findings shall never be based solely on opinion or unsupported observations.

# Objectives

The methodology is designed to:

- Standardize assessment findings.
- Ensure findings are supported by evidence.
- Support executive decision making.
- Identify root causes.
- Link findings to risks.
- Enable remediation planning.
- Maintain complete assessment traceability.
- Support future audits and reassessments.

---

# Definition of a Finding

A finding exists when sufficient evidence demonstrates one or more of the following:

- A required control does not exist.
- A control is improperly designed.
- A control has not been implemented.
- A control failed to operate effectively.
- Evidence is insufficient to support compliance.
- A material regulatory requirement is not satisfied.
- A significant cybersecurity risk remains unmanaged.
- Management has accepted risk beyond approved tolerance.
- A repeat deficiency exists.

---

# Finding Identification Standard

Each finding shall receive a permanent identifier.

Example:

```text
FIN-001
FIN-002
FIN-003
```

Identifiers shall:

- Never be reused
- Never change
- Be referenced throughout all assessment documentation
- Remain active even if the finding is later closed

# The Five-Part Finding Structure

Every professional audit or consulting finding should answer five questions.

## 1. Criteria

**What should exist?**

Examples:

- HIPAA Security Rule
- NIST CSF
- Internal Policy
- Organization Standard

---

## 2. Condition

**What actually exists?**

Describe factual observations supported by evidence.

---

## 3. Cause

**Why did the condition occur?**

Examples:

- Missing process
- Lack of ownership
- Budget limitation
- Technical debt
- Training deficiency
- Governance weakness

---

## 4. Effect

**What is the impact?**

Examples:

- Increased likelihood of breach
- HIPAA non-compliance
- Patient safety exposure
- Financial loss
- Regulatory action
- Operational disruption

---

## 5. Recommendation

**What should management do?**

Recommendations should:

- Be realistic
- Reduce risk
- Assign ownership
- Improve maturity
- Be measurable

# Executive Summary Requirements

Each finding should begin with a concise executive summary.

Example:

> Multi-factor authentication is not consistently enforced for privileged accounts, increasing the likelihood of unauthorized access to systems containing electronic protected health information (ePHI).

The executive summary should:

- Fit within one paragraph
- Describe business impact
- Avoid unnecessary technical language
- Stand alone

# Recommendation Standards

Recommendations should be:

- Actionable
- Prioritized
- Risk-based
- Practical
- Measurable
- Assigned

Avoid vague recommendations such as:

> Improve security.

Instead use:

> Implement MFA for all privileged accounts and integrate enforcement through centralized identity management.

---

Management responses should include:

- Agreement or disagreement

- Funding considerations
- Dependencies
- Accepted residual risk

# Repeat Findings

A repeat finding exists when:

- Previously identified deficiency remains unresolved.
- Corrective action failed.
- Similar weakness exists in another environment.
- Compensating controls failed.

Repeat findings should identify:

- Original assessment
- Original finding ID
- Time outstanding
- Reason for recurrence

---

# Writing Standards

Findings shall be:

- Objective
- Evidence-based
- Free of opinion
- Grammatically correct
- Concise
- Technically accurate
- Business focused

Avoid:

- Emotional language
- Blame
- Assumptions
- Unsupported conclusions

# Example Finding

## FIN-007

### Title

Privileged Accounts Are Not Protected by Multi-Factor Authentication

### Criteria

HIPAA Security Rule §164.312(d)

Organization Identity Standard

### Condition

Review of privileged accounts determined that 17 administrator accounts did not require MFA.

### Cause

Legacy infrastructure has not been integrated into centralized identity services.

### Effect

Compromise of administrator credentials could result in unauthorized access to ePHI systems.

### Risk Rating

Critical

### Recommendation

Require MFA for all privileged accounts and remove legacy authentication methods.

# Consultant Notes

## Best Practices

- Write findings for executives, not engineers.
- Focus on business impact.
- Keep observations factual.
- Make recommendations achievable.
- Link every finding to evidence.

## Common Pitfalls

- Writing overly technical findings.
- Blaming individuals.
- Mixing observations and opinions.
- Recommendations that are too vague.
- Missing traceability.

## Red Flags

- Findings without evidence.
- Recommendations without owners.
- Unsupported Critical ratings.
- Root causes listed as "human error."
- Findings that cannot be remediated.
