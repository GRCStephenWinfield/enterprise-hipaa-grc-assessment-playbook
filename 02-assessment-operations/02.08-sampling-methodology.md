# Purpose

This document establishes the methodology used to select and evaluate samples during the Enterprise HIPAA Cybersecurity Assessment.

Sampling allows the assessment team to evaluate control operation when reviewing an entire population would be impractical, inefficient, or unnecessary.

The methodology is designed to ensure that samples are selected consistently, are appropriate for the assessed risk, and provide enough support for defensible control conclusions.

# Objectives

This methodology is designed to:

- Establish consistent sample-selection practices.
- Ensure samples are appropriate for the control and risk.
- Define population-validation requirements.
- Support representative and defensible testing.
- Identify when full-population testing is required.
- Establish sample-expansion criteria.
- Prevent convenience sampling from weakening conclusions.
- Support consistent quality review.
- Protect clinical operations and sensitive information.

---

# Sampling Principles

Sampling shall be:

- Risk-based
- Documented
- Reproducible
- Appropriate to control frequency
- Appropriate to population size
- Sensitive to business and clinical criticality
- Supported by a validated population
- Expanded when exceptions indicate broader weakness
- Reviewed independently where required

Sampling does not eliminate the need for professional judgment.

# Population Definition

Before selecting a sample, the assessor shall document:

- Population description
- Population owner
- Source system
- Assessment period
- Population size
- Required data fields
- Inclusion criteria
- Exclusion criteria
- Known limitations
- Date extracted
- Method used to validate completeness

Example populations include:

- All terminated users during the assessment period
- All quarterly access reviews
- All Critical vulnerabilities identified
- All security incidents classified High or Critical
- All privileged accounts
- All production changes
- All critical vendors
- All backup-restoration tests
- All policy exceptions

---

# Population Validation

Population completeness and accuracy should be validated through one or more of the following:

- Reconciliation to an independent source
- Comparison with system totals
- Review of extraction criteria
- Inspection of source-system settings
- Reperformance of report generation
- Comparison to a secondary inventory
- Review of record sequence or timestamps
- Confirmation with the population owner
- Technical validation by the assessor

If the population cannot be validated, the limitation shall be documented and considered when determining the control conclusion.

# Sampling Methods

## Full-Population Testing

Review every item in the population.

Use when:

- The population is small.
- The control is Critical.
- Automated analysis makes full testing practical.
- Prior exceptions suggest systemic weakness.
- Regulatory significance is high.
- Patient-safety implications require complete review.
- The full population can be tested without disruption.

Examples:

- All privileged accounts
- All Critical vulnerabilities
- All High-severity incidents
- All policy exceptions
- All unencrypted endpoints
- All missed backup jobs for Tier 0 systems

---

## Random Sampling

Select items using a random method so every item has an equal chance of selection.

Use when:

- The population is relatively uniform.
- There is no reason to target specific records.
- A representative sample is appropriate.
- Population data is complete and usable.

The workpaper should document the randomization method.

---

## Systematic Sampling

Select items at a defined interval after choosing a starting point.

Example:

```text
Select every 20th record beginning with record 7.
```

Use when:

- The population is ordered.
- Records do not contain a repeating pattern that could bias selection.
- A reproducible method is needed.

---

## Judgmental Sampling

Select items based on risk, significance, or assessor judgment.

Use when:

- Certain items present greater risk.
- Critical assets require focused testing.
- Prior findings exist.
- Exceptions are likely to cluster.
- Regulatory or patient-safety significance is elevated.
- The population is not uniform.

Examples:

- Privileged users
- High-risk vendors
- Internet-facing systems
- Tier 0 clinical applications
- Emergency changes
- Repeat policy exceptions

Judgmental sampling should not be described as statistically representative.

---

## Stratified Sampling

Divide the population into meaningful groups and select items from each group.

Possible strata include:

- Criticality
- Location
- System type
- User type
- Department
- Risk level
- Control owner
- Technology platform
- Time period

Example:

```text
Select users from clinical, administrative, vendor, privileged, and remote-access populations.
```

---

## Attribute Sampling

Evaluate whether each sampled item satisfies a defined control attribute.

Examples:

- Access approval present
- Termination completed on time
- Patch installed within SLA
- Backup restoration successful
- Risk acceptance approved
- Vendor assessment completed

Results may be summarized using an exception rate.

---

## Targeted Sampling

Select specific items because of known risk indicators.

Examples:

- Accounts with recent privilege escalation
- Systems missing EDR coverage
- Vendors with expired assurance reports
- Vulnerabilities past due
- Incidents involving ePHI
- Failed recovery tests

Targeted sampling is used to investigate risk and should not be presented as representative of the entire population.

# Control Frequency and Sample Guidance

The following ranges are planning guidelines, not mandatory statistical requirements.

| Control Frequency | Population Example | Suggested Initial Sample |
|------------------|--------------------|--------------------------|
| Annual | 1 occurrence | Test the occurrence |
| Semiannual | 2 occurrences | Test both |
| Quarterly | 4 occurrences | Test 2–4 |
| Monthly | 12 occurrences | Test 3–6 |
| Weekly | Approximately 52 occurrences | Test 5–10 |
| Daily | Approximately 250–365 occurrences | Test 10–25 |
| Per Transaction | Large population | Risk-based sample, typically 15–30 |
| Continuous Automated | Configuration and exception population | Inspect configuration and test selected exceptions |
| Event-Driven | Incidents, terminations, changes | Sample based on risk and event count |

Higher-risk controls may require larger or full-population testing.

---

# Risk Factors Affecting Sample Size

Sample size should increase when:

- The control is Critical.
- The system supports patient care.
- ePHI exposure is possible.
- Prior findings exist.
- The process is manual.
- Multiple owners perform the control.
- The population is heterogeneous.
- Exceptions are expected.
- Evidence confidence is limited.
- The control recently changed.
- Population completeness is uncertain.
- The control operates across multiple facilities or platforms.

Sample size may be reduced when:

- Full-population automated testing is performed.
- The control is highly automated and configuration is centrally enforced.
- Strong independent assurance exists.
- The population is small and homogeneous.
- Prior results demonstrate sustained effectiveness.
- Risk is Low and consequences are limited.

# Sample Identifier Standard

Sample items may be assigned identifiers using:

```text
SMP-[DOMAIN]-[SEQUENCE]
```

Examples:

```text
SMP-IAM-001
SMP-VM-001
SMP-IR-001
```

When multiple items are tested under one control, use:

```text
SMP-IAM-001-01
SMP-IAM-001-02
SMP-IAM-001-03
```

---

# Sample Expansion Criteria

Testing should be expanded when:

- One or more exceptions involve Critical or High-risk assets.
- The exception rate exceeds the defined tolerance.
- Exceptions appear systemic.
- Population completeness is questionable.
- Evidence conflicts with interview statements.
- The same issue appears across multiple departments or systems.
- A prior finding may remain unresolved.
- A sample item reveals patient-safety or regulatory exposure.
- Additional testing is required to determine scope.
- The Quality Reviewer requests expansion.

---

# Sample Expansion Approaches

Possible expansion methods include:

- Increase the sample size.
- Test all items in the affected stratum.
- Test the full population.
- Add another time period.
- Select records from additional locations.
- Select records handled by different operators.
- Test additional systems.
- Perform technical validation.
- Obtain corroborating evidence.

The reason and outcome shall be documented.

---

# Exception Rate

Where appropriate, calculate:

```text
Exception Rate = Number of Exceptions ÷ Number of Items Tested × 100
```

Example:

```text
3 exceptions ÷ 25 items tested × 100 = 12%
```

The exception rate is one factor in evaluating effectiveness and should not replace consideration of severity, criticality, or systemic risk.

---

# Zero-Tolerance Controls

Some controls may require zero exceptions.

Examples include:

- MFA for privileged access
- Unauthorized access to ePHI
- Shared administrator accounts
- Unapproved production changes affecting patient safety
- Missing encryption for Restricted data without approved exception
- Unreviewed Critical security incidents
- Tier 0 backup restoration failure without escalation
- Unsupported emergency access without audit trail

A single exception may support a material finding.

---

# Automated Testing

Automated analysis may be used to:

- Evaluate full populations.
- Reconcile inventories.
- Identify duplicates.
- Compare configuration settings.
- Calculate SLA compliance.
- Detect outliers.
- Validate coverage.
- Identify overdue records.

Automated testing should document:

- Tool used
- Query or logic
- Input source
- Date performed
- Scope
- Validation steps
- Limitations
- Output reference

Automation output shall be reviewed for accuracy before use.

---

# Time-Period Coverage

Samples should cover the assessment period rather than cluster around one date.

Where practical, select items across:

- Beginning of period
- Middle of period
- End of period
- Different quarters
- Different operational cycles
- Peak and non-peak periods
- Periods before and after major changes

This helps determine whether the control operated consistently.

---

# Multi-Location and Multi-System Sampling

When a control operates across multiple facilities, systems, or business units, the sample should consider:

- Location criticality
- Different operators
- Different platforms
- Different configurations
- Centralized versus decentralized processes
- Known risk differences
- Historical findings
- Clinical versus administrative environments

A sample from one location shall not automatically support an enterprise-wide conclusion.

---

# Sampling Sensitive Information

When selecting records involving PHI or ePHI:

- Use redacted data where practical.
- Limit fields to those required for testing.
- Avoid downloading complete patient records.
- Use representative identifiers.
- Store samples in authorized repositories.
- Restrict access.
- Document why sensitive data was necessary.
- Follow approved retention and destruction requirements.

---

# Sampling Limitations

Possible limitations include:

- Incomplete population
- Restricted access
- Missing historical records
- Small population
- Vendor-controlled data
- Excessive redaction
- Unavailable system logs
- Non-reproducible reports
- Population changes during testing
- Duplicate or invalid records

Material limitations shall be reflected in the workpaper and conclusion.

---

# Sampling Quality Review

The Quality Reviewer should confirm:

- The population is clearly defined.
- Population completeness was evaluated.
- The sampling method is appropriate.
- The sample size is justified.
- The selection is reproducible.
- High-risk items are adequately represented.
- Exceptions are accurately recorded.
- Expansion decisions are reasonable.
- Sensitive data is protected.
- The conclusion does not exceed what the sample supports.

# Consultant Notes

## Best Practices

- Validate the population before selecting a sample.
- Use full-population testing when automation makes it practical.
- Include high-risk records even when random sampling is used.
- Spread selections across the assessment period.
- Expand testing when exceptions suggest systemic weakness.
- Document the rationale before testing.
- Avoid overstating conclusions from small samples.

## Common Pitfalls

- Selecting records because they are easy to obtain.
- Using a sample from one system to conclude enterprise-wide effectiveness.
- Failing to verify population completeness.
- Treating statistical and judgmental sampling as equivalent.
- Ignoring a severe exception because the overall exception rate is low.
- Changing the sample after seeing results without documenting why.
- Allowing control owners to choose all sample items.

## Red Flags

- The population cannot be reproduced.
- Sample records were prepared specifically for the assessment.
- High-risk items were excluded without explanation.
- Exceptions cluster around one team, location, or period.
- Manual records differ from source-system data.
- Management requests replacement of failed sample items.
- A Critical control is tested using only a minimal convenience sample.
