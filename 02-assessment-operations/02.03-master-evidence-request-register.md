# Purpose

The Master Evidence Request Register identifies, assigns, tracks, validates, and closes all documentation and technical evidence requested during the Enterprise HIPAA Cybersecurity Assessment.

This register serves as the authoritative record of:

- Evidence requested
- Evidence ownership
- Request and due dates
- Submission status
- Evidence quality
- Assessment-domain usage
- Follow-up requirements
- Evidence acceptance or rejection
- Storage and retention references

The register supports traceability from an applicable requirement to the evidence reviewed, assessment procedure performed, conclusion reached, finding documented, and risk reported.

# Evidence Management Objectives

The evidence-management process is designed to:

- Collect sufficient and appropriate evidence.
- Avoid unnecessary collection of PHI and ePHI.
- Maintain evidence confidentiality and integrity.
- Assign evidence to accountable custodians.
- Track overdue or incomplete submissions.
- Prevent unsupported assessment conclusions.
- Document evidence limitations.
- Support quality assurance and defensibility.
- Maintain traceability throughout the assessment lifecycle.

---

# Evidence Identification Standard

Each evidence request shall receive a unique identifier using the following format:

```text
EVD-001
EVD-002
EVD-003
```

Identifiers shall:

- Remain unique throughout the engagement.
- Never be reused.
- Remain stable after assignment.
- Be referenced in workpapers, findings, risks, and reports.
- Be marked cancelled rather than deleted when no longer required.

Supplemental submissions may use a suffix:

```text
EVD-014-A
EVD-014-B
```

# Evidence Quality Criteria

Evidence will be evaluated against the following criteria:

| Criterion | Assessment Question |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Relevance | Does the evidence directly address the control or requirement? |
| Reliability | Was it produced by a trustworthy and appropriate source? |
| Completeness | Does it include all required records, periods, and attributes? |
| Accuracy | Is the content internally consistent and technically credible? |
| Timeliness | Does it cover the required assessment period? |
| Authenticity | Can the origin and integrity of the evidence be validated? |
| Sufficiency | Is there enough evidence to support the conclusion? |
| Traceability | Can it be linked to the system, control, requirement, and test? |

---

# Evidence Confidence Ratings

| Rating | Definition |
|--------|------------|
| 🟢 High | Corroborated by multiple reliable sources, technical validation, observation, or reperformance |
| 🟡 Medium | Supported by one reliable source or partially corroborated evidence |
| 🔴 Low | Based primarily on inquiry, incomplete information, or unverified documentation |

A low-confidence conclusion shall not be reported as definitive without clearly documenting the limitation.

---

# Evidence Handling Requirements

Evidence shall be:

- Transmitted through approved secure channels.
- Stored only in authorized assessment repositories.
- Restricted using least-privilege access.
- Protected according to its data classification.
- Referenced by identifier rather than duplicated unnecessarily.
- Reviewed only by authorized personnel.
- Retained according to approved engagement requirements.
- Securely destroyed when retention obligations expire.

The assessment team should avoid collecting live PHI or ePHI whenever practical.

Preferred alternatives include:

- Redacted records
- Sanitized screenshots
- Sample identifiers
- Aggregated reports
- Non-production demonstrations
- Configuration exports without patient data

---

# Prohibited Evidence Practices

The assessment team shall not:

- Request unnecessary patient records.
- Store evidence in personal email or consumer storage.
- Copy authentication secrets into workpapers.
- Retain unredacted PHI without authorization.
- Modify original evidence.
- Accept undocumented verbal assertions as conclusive evidence.
- Share evidence outside the approved assessment team.
- Delete evidence requests to conceal missing submissions.
- Use production access beyond the approved purpose.

# Follow-Up Request Process

A follow-up request should be issued when:

- The submission does not answer the original request.
- Required periods or populations are missing.
- The evidence is outdated.
- Screenshots lack dates or identifying context.
- Reports cannot be tied to a source system.
- Evidence conflicts with stakeholder statements.
- Evidence reveals a possible control deficiency.
- Redaction prevents meaningful review.
- Additional corroboration is required.

Follow-up submissions shall reference the original evidence ID.

Example:

```text
EVD-016-A — Updated MFA Coverage Report
```

# Consultant Notes

## Best Practices

- Request evidence in prioritized waves rather than sending every request simultaneously.
- Explain the purpose of unusual or burdensome requests.
- Review evidence before interviews so discussions can focus on gaps and clarifications.
- Prefer source-system exports over manually prepared summaries.
- Retain the original submission and document any transformed working copy.
- Use evidence identifiers in all workpapers and findings.

## Common Pitfalls

- Accepting screenshots without dates, context, or source identification.
- Treating policy documents as proof of operating effectiveness.
- Requesting excessive sensitive data.
- Allowing evidence to remain in email.
- Failing to document why evidence was rejected.
- Closing a domain despite material unresolved evidence requests.

## Red Flags

- Repeated claims that expected evidence does not exist.
- Manually created reports that cannot be reproduced.
- Contradictions between evidence sources.
- Evidence generated only after the request and presented as historical proof.
- Unexplained missing periods or records.
- Requests to avoid documenting evidence limitations.
- Unapproved PHI or credential information submitted as evidence.
