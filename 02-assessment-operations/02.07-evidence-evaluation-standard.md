# Purpose

This standard establishes the methodology used to evaluate evidence collected during the Enterprise HIPAA Cybersecurity Assessment.

The standard defines how assessors determine whether evidence is relevant, reliable, complete, accurate, timely, authentic, sufficient, and traceable enough to support control-testing results, findings, risk conclusions, and executive reporting.

Evidence shall be evaluated consistently across all assessment domains.

# Objectives

This standard is designed to:

- Establish consistent evidence-quality criteria.
- Prevent unsupported assessment conclusions.
- Distinguish evidence existence from evidence sufficiency.
- Identify incomplete, outdated, unreliable, or conflicting evidence.
- Support evidence-confidence ratings.
- Protect PHI, ePHI, credentials, and sensitive technical information.
- Maintain traceability from requirements through final reporting.
- Document material evidence limitations.
- Support independent quality review.

---

# Evidence Evaluation Principles

Assessment evidence should be:

- Relevant to the requirement being evaluated
- Obtained from an appropriate source
- Complete enough to support the intended procedure
- Accurate and internally consistent
- Timely for the assessment period
- Authentic and attributable
- Sufficient to support the conclusion
- Traceable to the control, system, process, and workpaper

The mere existence of a document does not demonstrate that a control operated effectively.

# Evidence Evaluation Criteria

## 1. Relevance

Evidence is relevant when it directly supports the requirement, control, system, process, or assessment objective being evaluated.

Questions include:

- Does the evidence address the requested control?
- Does it relate to the correct system or process?
- Does it cover the required population?
- Does it demonstrate design, implementation, or operation?
- Is it responsive to the original evidence request?

Evidence may be genuine but still irrelevant to the intended procedure.

---

## 2. Reliability

Evidence is reliable when it originates from a credible source and was produced through a controlled process.

Higher-reliability evidence may include:

- Source-system exports
- System-generated reports
- Approved records
- Independently reviewed reports
- Technically validated configurations
- Reperformed results

Lower-reliability evidence may include:

- Manually prepared summaries
- Undated screenshots
- Uncorroborated interview statements
- Informal spreadsheets
- Documents without ownership or approval
- Reports that cannot be reproduced

---

## 3. Completeness

Evidence is complete when it includes all information necessary to evaluate the control.

Questions include:

- Does it cover the full assessment period?
- Does it represent the complete population?
- Are required fields present?
- Are pages or sections missing?
- Are exclusions documented?
- Can the assessor determine whether records were omitted?
- Are supporting approvals included?

Incomplete evidence may require supplemental requests or a limitation.

---

## 4. Accuracy

Evidence is accurate when the information is technically and logically correct.

Assessors should consider:

- Whether calculations are correct
- Whether totals reconcile
- Whether dates are consistent
- Whether system names and identifiers are accurate
- Whether the evidence conflicts with other sources
- Whether reported results can be reproduced
- Whether manual entries contain errors

---

## 5. Timeliness

Evidence is timely when it applies to the defined assessment period and reflects the environment being assessed.

Evidence may be outdated when:

- It predates major system changes.
- It reflects a retired control.
- It covers the wrong period.
- It was generated after fieldwork without showing historical operation.
- Policies have expired or passed their review dates.
- Current operations differ materially from the evidence.

---

## 6. Authenticity

Evidence is authentic when its origin and integrity can be reasonably established.

Authenticity indicators include:

- Source-system metadata
- System-generated timestamps
- Digital signatures
- Approval records
- Repository history
- File properties
- Consistent naming conventions
- Confirmed ownership

Evidence should not be altered in a way that obscures its original content.

---

## 7. Sufficiency

Evidence is sufficient when there is enough appropriate support to reach the intended conclusion.

Sufficiency depends on:

- Control risk
- Control frequency
- Sample size
- Evidence reliability
- Population completeness
- Exceptions identified
- Corroboration
- Business and regulatory significance

Critical controls generally require stronger and more diverse evidence than low-risk controls.

---

## 8. Traceability

Evidence is traceable when it can be linked to:

- Authority or requirement
- Control ID
- Evidence request ID
- System or business-process ID
- Interview ID
- Workpaper ID
- Test procedure
- Finding ID
- Risk ID
- Recommendation ID
- Corrective action or POA&M item

# Evidence Reliability Hierarchy

| Level | Evidence Source | General Reliability |
|------:|-----------------|---------------------|
| 1 | Independent reperformance or direct technical validation | Very High |
| 2 | Source-system exports, logs, and automated reports | High |
| 3 | Approved records, signed reports, and controlled repositories | High |
| 4 | Third-party assurance reports and certifications | Moderate to High |
| 5 | Screenshots with date, source, and contextual identifiers | Moderate |
| 6 | Manually prepared summaries supported by source records | Moderate |
| 7 | Interview representations with partial corroboration | Low to Moderate |
| 8 | Unsupported verbal or written assertions | Low |

The hierarchy provides guidance but does not replace professional judgment.

# Evidence Confidence Ratings

## High Confidence

Assign **High** confidence when:

- Evidence comes from reliable sources.
- The full period or required population is covered.
- Material information is corroborated.
- Technical validation or reperformance supports the result.
- No significant contradictions remain.
- Traceability is complete.

---

## Medium Confidence

Assign **Medium** confidence when:

- Evidence is relevant but limited.
- Only one reliable source is available.
- Some attributes or periods are incomplete.
- Corroboration is partial.
- Minor questions remain unresolved.
- The evidence is adequate only for a qualified conclusion.

---

## Low Confidence

Assign **Low** confidence when:

- Evidence is primarily based on inquiry.
- The source cannot be validated.
- Material periods or records are missing.
- Evidence conflicts with other sources.
- Population completeness cannot be established.
- The evidence was created only after the request.
- Significant limitations remain unresolved.

---

# Evidence Decision Matrix

| Relevance | Reliability | Completeness | Decision |
|-----------|-------------|--------------|----------|
| High | High | Complete | Accept |
| High | Medium | Complete | Accept with qualification or follow-up |
| High | High | Partial | Follow-up required |
| Medium | High | Complete | Determine whether supplemental evidence is needed |
| Low | Any | Any | Reject |
| High | Low | Partial | Reject or record limitation |
| Conflicting | Any | Any | Investigate and reconcile |

# Evidence Acceptance Status

## Accepted

Use when:

- Evidence satisfies the intended request.
- Required attributes are present.
- Quality is appropriate for the control risk.
- Material limitations have been resolved.
- Evidence can be used in the workpaper.

---

## Accepted with Limitation

Use when:

- Evidence supports part of the procedure.
- A qualified conclusion is necessary.
- Minor limitations remain.
- Supplemental evidence is unavailable but the remaining support is usable.

The limitation must be documented in the workpaper.

---

## Follow-Up Required

Use when:

- Additional context is needed.
- Missing records may be obtainable.
- The wrong period was submitted.
- The population is incomplete.
- A technical discrepancy must be explained.
- Additional corroboration is needed.

---

## Rejected

Use when:

- Evidence is unrelated.
- The source is unreliable.
- The evidence cannot be authenticated.
- Material information is missing.
- The evidence does not satisfy the request.
- The submission contains prohibited sensitive information that cannot be retained.

---

## Not Available

Use when:

- The evidence does not exist.
- The organization cannot produce it.
- The source system does not retain the required records.
- A vendor controls the evidence and will not provide it.

The absence of expected evidence may support a finding.

# Screenshot Evaluation Requirements

Screenshots should include, where practical:

- System name
- Relevant page or configuration
- Date and time
- User or role context
- Complete setting or result
- Visible filters
- Relevant identifiers
- Sufficient surrounding context

Screenshots should not include unnecessary:

- PHI or ePHI
- Passwords
- API keys
- Authentication tokens
- Private encryption keys
- Personal financial information
- Unrelated confidential data

A cropped image without source or context should not be treated as high-confidence evidence.

---

# Report Evaluation Requirements

Reports should be evaluated for:

- Source system
- Generation date
- Reporting period
- Filters applied
- Complete population
- Record count
- Required attributes
- Ownership
- Reproducibility
- Reconciliation to other sources

Manually created reports should be validated against source-system records where practical.

---

# Policy and Procedure Evaluation

When reviewing policies or procedures, assessors should determine:

- Whether the document is approved
- Whether ownership is assigned
- Whether the review date is current
- Whether the scope is appropriate
- Whether responsibilities are defined
- Whether requirements are measurable
- Whether exceptions are governed
- Whether operating practices match the document
- Whether related standards and procedures exist
- Whether implementation evidence is available

A policy alone demonstrates governance intent, not operating effectiveness.

---

# Technical Configuration Evidence

Technical configuration evidence should include:

- System identifier
- Environment
- Configuration source
- Date captured
- Relevant settings
- Scope or coverage
- Applied policies
- Exceptions
- Change history where relevant
- Assessor validation steps

Configuration evidence should be obtained through approved read-only methods whenever possible.

---

# Third-Party Evidence

Third-party evidence may include:

- SOC reports
- ISO certificates
- HITRUST reports
- Penetration-test summaries
- Business continuity reports
- Vendor assessments
- Security questionnaires
- Contracts and BAAs

Assessors should evaluate:

- Scope
- Reporting period
- Exceptions
- Subservice organizations
- Complementary user-entity controls
- Report age
- Applicability to services used by MRMC
- Management responses
- Whether material gaps require follow-up

A certification badge or marketing statement is insufficient evidence.

---

# Interview Evidence

Interview statements should be classified as:

| Classification | Meaning |
|----------------|---------|
| Corroborated | Supported by reliable evidence |
| Partially Corroborated | Some supporting evidence exists |
| Uncorroborated | No supporting evidence obtained |
| Contradicted | Other evidence conflicts with the statement |
| Not Testable | No reasonable validation method exists |

Inquiry alone should not support an Effective conclusion for a recurring or technical control when operating evidence should exist.

---

# Conflicting Evidence

When evidence conflicts:

1. Record each source.
2. Determine whether the sources cover the same period and scope.
3. Validate system and report identifiers.
4. Interview the responsible owners.
5. Request source-system evidence.
6. Determine whether the conflict indicates a control issue.
7. Document unresolved contradictions.
8. Consider expanding testing.
9. Escalate material discrepancies.

The assessor shall not select the most favorable source without documented justification.

---

# Evidence Created After the Request

Evidence created after the request may demonstrate current implementation but may not prove historical operation.

The assessor should determine:

- Whether the document existed during the assessment period
- Whether metadata supports historical use
- Whether related records corroborate operation
- Whether the evidence represents remediation performed during the assessment
- Whether the control should be rated based on the pre-remediation or current state

Remediation performed during fieldwork should be documented separately from historical control effectiveness.

---

# Evidence Limitations

Material evidence limitations include:

- Missing records
- Incomplete populations
- Unavailable historical evidence
- Restricted access
- Vendor-controlled evidence
- Unreliable manual reports
- Inconsistent sources
- Excessive redaction
- Inability to reproduce reports
- Inadequate sample support

Limitations should be linked to the applicable workpaper and assessment conclusion.

---

# Evidence Escalation Criteria

Escalation is required when:

- Critical evidence is more than five business days overdue.
- A control owner refuses to provide expected evidence.
- Evidence suggests active compromise.
- Evidence contains potentially reportable ePHI exposure.
- Evidence appears altered or misleading.
- Conflicting evidence materially affects conclusions.
- Missing evidence prevents completion of a critical control test.
- Sensitive evidence was submitted through an unauthorized channel.

---

# Evidence Retention and Disposal

Evidence retention shall consider:

- Contractual requirements
- Legal-hold obligations
- Regulatory requirements
- Client policies
- Assessment defensibility
- Sensitive-data classifications
- Approved destruction schedules

Evidence shall not be retained longer than necessary without a documented requirement.

Secure disposal methods should be appropriate to the evidence format and classification.

---

# Quality Assurance Requirements

The Quality Reviewer should verify that:

- Evidence decisions are consistent.
- Critical conclusions use appropriate evidence.
- High-confidence ratings are justified.
- Limitations are disclosed.
- Evidence is traceable to workpapers.
- Rejected submissions include documented reasons.
- PHI and credentials are handled appropriately.
- Conflicting sources are resolved or disclosed.
- Findings are supported by accepted evidence.
- No final conclusion relies solely on unsupported inquiry.

# Consultant Notes

## Best Practices

- Evaluate evidence before using it in a workpaper.
- Prefer source-system evidence over manually prepared summaries.
- Document why evidence is sufficient, not merely that it exists.
- Corroborate critical conclusions through multiple sources.
- Record evidence limitations immediately.
- Preserve the original submission.
- Separate historical evidence from remediation completed during fieldwork.

## Common Pitfalls

- Accepting screenshots without context.
- Treating policies as proof of operation.
- Ignoring missing periods.
- Accepting manually altered reports without validation.
- Assigning High confidence without corroboration.
- Failing to disclose evidence limitations.
- Allowing unsupported inquiry to drive final conclusions.

## Red Flags

- Evidence cannot be reproduced.
- Metadata conflicts with stated dates.
- Multiple sources contain materially different totals.
- Requested historical evidence appears newly created.
- Evidence owners repeatedly avoid source-system exports.
- Reports exclude known problem populations.
- The organization requests that missing evidence not be documented.
- Sensitive information is submitted through unapproved channels.
