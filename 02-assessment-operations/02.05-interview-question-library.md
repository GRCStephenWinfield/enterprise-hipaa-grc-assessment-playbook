# Purpose

The Interview Question Library provides a standardized collection of questions used during stakeholder interviews for the Enterprise HIPAA Cybersecurity Assessment.

The library promotes consistent inquiry across assessment domains while allowing assessors to tailor questions to each stakeholder’s responsibilities, systems, processes, and level of authority.

Interview questions support—but do not replace—evidence review, observation, testing, or reperformance.

# Library Objectives

The Interview Question Library is designed to:

- Promote consistent interview quality.
- Confirm control ownership and accountability.
- Understand documented and actual operating practices.
- Identify process and evidence gaps.
- Explore business and clinical impacts.
- Clarify conflicting information.
- Support control-design and operating-effectiveness conclusions.
- Generate follow-up evidence requests.
- Improve cross-domain consistency.
- Support defensible findings and recommendations.

Question identifiers may be referenced in interview notes and workpapers.

# Interview Question Principles

Assessors should:

- Begin with open-ended questions.
- Ask stakeholders to explain processes in their own words.
- Request demonstrations when appropriate.
- Avoid leading questions.
- Distinguish documented policy from actual practice.
- Ask how exceptions are handled.
- Explore monitoring and oversight.
- Request examples from the assessment period.
- Confirm ownership and escalation paths.
- Corroborate material statements through evidence.

# Standard Opening Questions

Use these questions to establish context at the beginning of most interviews.

1. Please describe your role and responsibilities.
2. Which systems, processes, or controls do you own?
3. Who is accountable for the final outcome of this process?
4. Which teams support or depend upon this process?
5. What are the most important business or clinical outcomes supported by this process?
6. What laws, regulations, policies, or standards govern your responsibilities?
7. What documentation describes the process?
8. How has the process changed during the assessment period?
9. What are the most significant risks or challenges?
10. What evidence is available to demonstrate the process operates as intended?

# Sensitive Interview Topics

The Lead Assessor should coordinate with Legal, Privacy, Human Resources, or Compliance before discussing:

- Suspected misconduct
- Fraud
- Workforce sanctions
- Active investigations
- Regulatory inquiries
- Potential breaches
- Patient-safety events
- Litigation
- Whistleblower allegations
- Personal performance concerns

Sensitive topics should be documented using restricted workpapers.

# Interview Question Selection Process

For each interview:

1. Confirm the stakeholder’s responsibilities.
2. Identify applicable domains.
3. Review available evidence.
4. Select core questions.
5. Add risk-specific questions.
6. Add evidence-clarification questions.
7. Add follow-up questions from prior interviews.
8. Remove questions outside the stakeholder’s responsibilities.
9. Confirm sensitive-topic handling.
10. Record selected question IDs in the interview workpaper.

# Interview Question Quality Standards

Questions should be:

- Clear
- Neutral
- Relevant
- Open-ended where appropriate
- Traceable to an assessment objective
- Appropriate for the stakeholder
- Focused on both design and operation
- Capable of producing actionable information
- Free from unnecessary technical jargon
- Respectful of clinical and operational priorities

# Consultant Notes

## Best Practices

- Use the library as a guide, not a script.
- Ask follow-up questions based on unexpected answers.
- Request demonstrations whenever practical.
- Compare management descriptions with operational reality.
- Ask for recent examples rather than hypothetical explanations.
- Record question identifiers in interview notes.

## Common Pitfalls

- Asking every stakeholder the same questions.
- Allowing time pressure to eliminate critical follow-up questions.
- Asking highly technical questions of executive stakeholders.
- Treating “yes” answers as sufficient.
- Failing to distinguish policy from practice.
- Avoiding uncomfortable but relevant questions.

## Red Flags

- Stakeholders provide rehearsed but unsupported answers.
- Ownership remains unclear after repeated questioning.
- Participants refuse demonstrations without explanation.
- Different teams describe contradictory processes.
- Evidence is repeatedly promised but not produced.
- Significant issues are described as “known” but not tracked.
- Stakeholders ask that material responses remain off the record.
