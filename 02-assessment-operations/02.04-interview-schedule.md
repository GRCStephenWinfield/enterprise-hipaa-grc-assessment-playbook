# Purpose

The Interview Schedule identifies, sequences, coordinates, and tracks all stakeholder interviews required during the Enterprise HIPAA Cybersecurity Assessment.

Interviews help the assessment team understand how governance processes, business activities, clinical workflows, and cybersecurity controls are designed and operated.

Interview statements support the assessment but do not replace objective evidence. Material representations should be corroborated through documentation, technical evidence, observation, or control testing whenever such evidence should reasonably exist.

# Interview Objectives

Stakeholder interviews are designed to:

- Confirm organizational and control ownership.
- Understand documented and actual operating practices.
- Clarify policies, standards, procedures, and technical evidence.
- Identify undocumented processes and dependencies.
- Understand clinical and business impacts.
- Validate control design and implementation.
- Identify additional evidence requirements.
- Explore potential control gaps.
- Support risk and maturity conclusions.
- Confirm management responses and remediation plans.

---

# Interview Identification Standard

Each interview shall receive a unique identifier using the following format:

```text
INT-001
INT-002
INT-003
```

Identifiers shall:

- Remain unique throughout the engagement.
- Be referenced in workpapers and evidence follow-up.
- Remain stable after assignment.
- Never be reused.
- Be marked cancelled rather than deleted.

Follow-up sessions may use a suffix:

```text
INT-009-A
INT-009-B
```

# Scheduling Principles

Interviews should be scheduled according to the following principles:

- Executive interviews occur early enough to establish business and risk context.
- Evidence should be reviewed before technical interviews whenever practical.
- Related stakeholders may be interviewed together when doing so improves efficiency.
- Sensitive topics should be addressed in appropriately restricted sessions.
- Clinical responsibilities take priority over assessment scheduling.
- Sessions should avoid shift changes, high-volume clinical periods, and major operational events.
- Follow-up interviews should be scheduled promptly.
- Required participants should receive sufficient preparation time.
- Assessor questions should be tailored to stakeholder responsibilities.
- Interview notes should be completed within one business day.

---

# Standard Interview Participants

Each interview should normally include:

- Lead interviewer
- Supporting assessor or note-taker
- Primary stakeholder
- Relevant control owner
- Client coordinator when appropriate
- Legal, privacy, or compliance representative for sensitive discussions when required

The number of participants should be limited to those necessary for an effective session.

# Interview Preparation Requirements

At least two business days before each interview, participants should receive:

- Interview title
- Purpose
- Planned duration
- Required attendees
- Assessment domain
- High-level discussion topics
- Relevant evidence requests
- Preparation instructions
- Secure meeting details

Sensitive findings or allegations should not be included in routine interview invitations.

---

# Interview Agenda Template

```text
1. Introductions and purpose
2. Confirm participant roles
3. Review process or control objective
4. Discuss documented procedures
5. Discuss actual operating practices
6. Review available evidence
7. Identify exceptions or limitations
8. Confirm ownership and monitoring
9. Identify follow-up evidence
10. Summarize next steps
```

# Interview Evidence Standard

Stakeholder representations should be classified as:

| Classification | Meaning |
|----------------|---------|
| Corroborated | Supported by reliable evidence |
| Partially Corroborated | Some evidence exists, but validation is incomplete |
| Uncorroborated | Statement has not been supported by evidence |
| Contradicted | Available evidence conflicts with the statement |
| Not Testable | The statement cannot reasonably be validated |

Uncorroborated statements should not be treated as proof of operating effectiveness.

---

# Interview Confidence Ratings

| Rating | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| 🟢 High | Responses are consistent with documentation, technical evidence, and other interviews |
| 🟡 Medium | Responses appear credible but have limited corroboration |
| 🔴 Low | Responses are unsupported, inconsistent, or contradicted by other evidence |

---

# Follow-Up Process

A follow-up interview or written clarification should be initiated when:

- Responses conflict with evidence.
- Ownership is unclear.
- A material process is undocumented.
- Additional evidence is required.
- Statements conflict across stakeholders.
- A possible finding requires factual validation.
- The interview identifies a new system, process, or risk.
- Significant questions remain unresolved.

Follow-up sessions shall reference the original interview ID.

# Interview Scheduling Constraints

The schedule should account for:

- Clinical shifts and patient-care responsibilities
- Executive calendars
- Change-freeze periods
- Regulatory deadlines
- Emergency operations
- Vendor availability
- Time-zone differences
- Union or workforce-representation requirements
- Legal or privacy participation needs
- Sensitive subject matter

---

# Interview Confidentiality

Interview participants should be informed that:

- Notes will be used for assessment purposes.
- Material statements may be validated through evidence.
- Confidential information will be restricted appropriately.
- Personal opinions should not be attributed beyond the authorized report structure.
- Evidence of active compromise or material regulatory risk may require immediate escalation.
- Assessors cannot agree to conceal material findings.

---

# Interview Quality Assurance

The Quality Reviewer should verify that:

- Required stakeholders were interviewed.
- Interview objectives align with the assessment scope.
- Notes are sufficiently detailed.
- Claims are linked to evidence where possible.
- Contradictions are resolved or documented.
- Follow-up actions are tracked.
- Conclusions do not rely solely on inquiry.
- Sensitive information is handled appropriately.

# Consultant Notes

## Best Practices

- Review available evidence before interviewing control owners.
- Use open-ended questions before asking detailed confirmation questions.
- Ask stakeholders to demonstrate processes rather than only describe them.
- Interview both management and operational personnel when feasible.
- Document contradictions without attempting to resolve them informally.
- Conclude each session by confirming follow-up items and owners.

## Common Pitfalls

- Reading questions mechanically without exploring unexpected responses.
- Accepting verbal statements as proof.
- Scheduling interviews before relevant evidence is available.
- Combining unrelated domains into oversized meetings.
- Failing to document who made a material statement.
- Allowing group interviews to hide unclear ownership.

## Red Flags

- Stakeholders repeatedly avoid direct questions.
- Different teams describe the same process inconsistently.
- Documented procedures do not match operating practices.
- Participants cannot identify control ownership.
- Requested demonstrations are repeatedly postponed.
- Management pressures assessors to change notes.
- Interviews reveal previously undisclosed systems, vendors, or ePHI flows.
