# Purpose

This workpaper evaluates whether Magnolia Regional Medical Center integrates asset information into its Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) program.

The assessment determines whether asset ownership, classification, criticality, lifecycle status, vulnerability exposure, and business impact are incorporated into enterprise risk assessments, executive reporting, and cybersecurity decision-making.

---

# Control Objective

Determine whether the organization:

- Assigns business criticality to enterprise assets.
- Integrates asset data into enterprise risk assessments.
- Prioritizes vulnerabilities using asset criticality.
- Incorporates asset information into Business Impact Analyses (BIAs).
- Supports executive decision-making with asset-based risk information.
- Updates enterprise risks when asset status changes.
- Maintains consistent asset-to-risk traceability.

# Risks Addressed

Failure to integrate assets into enterprise risk management may result in:

- Critical assets receiving inadequate protection.
- Vulnerability remediation being poorly prioritized.
- Inaccurate enterprise risk assessments.
- Weak executive decision-making.
- Business Impact Analyses lacking critical asset information.
- Misaligned cybersecurity investments.
- Increased operational risk.
- Regulatory noncompliance.

# Evaluation Criteria

### Effective

- Asset criticality drives enterprise risk decisions.
- Risk assessments reference enterprise assets.
- Vulnerability prioritization is risk-based.
- Executive reporting incorporates asset risk.
- Strong integration across governance processes.

### Partially Effective

- Asset risk integration exists.
- Some asset relationships missing.
- Limited executive reporting.
- BIA integration incomplete.

### Ineffective

- Asset management isolated from ERM.
- Risk assessments ignore asset criticality.
- Executive reporting lacks asset information.
- Vulnerability prioritization inconsistent.
