# Purpose

This workpaper evaluates whether Magnolia Regional Medical Center maintains a complete, accurate, and continuously updated inventory of medical devices and Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) assets.

The assessment determines whether connected medical devices are inventoried, assigned ownership, classified according to business criticality, monitored for security risks, and managed throughout their lifecycle to protect patient safety and support regulatory compliance.

---

# Control Objective

Determine whether the organization:

- Maintains a complete inventory of medical devices.
- Identifies all network-connected IoMT assets.
- Assigns ownership and custodianship.
- Maintains lifecycle documentation.
- Identifies unsupported or end-of-life devices.
- Integrates medical devices into cybersecurity monitoring.
- Supports vulnerability and risk management activities.

# Risks Addressed

Failure to maintain an accurate medical device inventory may result in:

- Unknown medical devices on the network.
- Unsupported clinical systems.
- Increased ransomware exposure.
- Delayed vulnerability remediation.
- Patient safety risks.
- Regulatory noncompliance.
- Inadequate incident response.
- Increased operational disruption.

# Evaluation Criteria

### Effective

- Complete medical device inventory.
- Accurate lifecycle records.
- Vendor support tracked.
- Cybersecurity monitoring integrated.
- End-of-life devices managed.
- Strong patient safety governance.

### Partially Effective

- Inventory maintained.
- Minor documentation gaps.
- Limited IoMT monitoring.
- Inconsistent lifecycle tracking.

### Ineffective

- Incomplete inventory.
- Unknown connected devices.
- Unsupported devices unmanaged.
- No cybersecurity integration.
- Significant patient safety exposure.
