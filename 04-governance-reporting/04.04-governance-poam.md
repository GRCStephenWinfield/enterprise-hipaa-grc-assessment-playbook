# Purpose

This Governance Plan of Action and Milestones (POA&M) establishes the formal remediation plan for reportable findings and risks identified during the Governance Domain Assessment.

The POA&M converts approved recommendations into:

- Defined corrective actions
- Accountable ownership
- Measurable milestones
- Target completion dates
- Required resources
- Dependencies
- Validation requirements
- Residual-risk decisions
- Executive reporting obligations

This document serves as the authoritative remediation tracker for Governance findings requiring formal corrective action.

# POA&M Objectives

The Governance POA&M is designed to:

- Resolve reportable Governance findings.
- Reduce documented residual risk.
- Establish clear remediation accountability.
- Define measurable corrective-action milestones.
- Monitor schedule, cost, and dependency risks.
- Provide executive visibility into remediation status.
- Support audit and regulatory defensibility.
- Require independent validation before closure.
- Prevent premature or unsupported finding closure.
- Advance Governance maturity toward Level 5.

---

# POA&M Identification Standard

Each Governance POA&M item shall receive a permanent identifier using:

```text
POAM-GOV-###
```

Examples:

```text
POAM-GOV-001
POAM-GOV-002
POAM-GOV-003
```

POA&M identifiers shall:

- Remain unique.
- Never be reused.
- Remain stable throughout remediation.
- Be referenced in findings, risks, recommendations, management responses, status reports, and closure records.
- Remain in the historical register after closure.

| Current Status | In Progress |
| Residual Risk Before Remediation | 12 — High |
| Target Residual Risk | 6 — Moderate |
| Executive Oversight | Required |

## Corrective-Action Objective

Eliminate indefinite cybersecurity risk acceptance and establish a governed lifecycle requiring expiration, reassessment, compensating-control validation, reauthorization, and escalation.

## Immediate Containment Actions

Within 30 days:

- Identify all active cybersecurity risk acceptances.
- Assign interim review dates.
- Escalate accepted High and Critical risks.
- Confirm accountable owners.
- Identify any acceptance without compensating controls.

Within 60 days:

- Reassess all High and Critical accepted risks.
- Validate compensating controls.
- Close, remediate, or reauthorize each acceptance.
- Report results to the Executive Risk Committee.

## Milestones

| Milestone ID | Milestone | Owner | Target | Completion Evidence | Status |
|--------------|-----------|-------|--------|---------------------|--------|
| GOV-003-M1 | Inventory all active risk acceptances | Enterprise Risk Manager | Day 15 | Reconciled acceptance inventory | Complete |
| GOV-003-M2 | Assign expiration and review dates | Risk Owners | Day 30 | Updated risk register | In Progress |
| GOV-003-M3 | Reassess High and Critical acceptances | CISO / Risk Owners | Day 60 | Reassessment records | Not Started |
| GOV-003-M4 | Revise Risk Acceptance Standard | Enterprise Risk Manager | Day 75 | Approved standard | Not Started |
| GOV-003-M5 | Configure automated reminders and escalation | GRC Administrator | Day 90 | Workflow evidence | Not Started |
| GOV-003-M6 | Report acceptance aging to executives | CISO | Day 105 | Executive report | Not Started |
| GOV-003-M7 | Retest process and close finding | Quality Reviewer | Day 120 | Retest workpaper and approval | Not Started |

## Required Control Requirements

The revised process shall require:

- Expiration date
- Maximum acceptance period
- Review frequency based on residual risk
- Business justification
- Named risk owner
- Compensating controls
- Compensating-control validation
- Residual-risk reassessment
- Formal reauthorization
- Executive escalation for overdue items
- Closure evidence
- Prohibition of indefinite High or Critical risk acceptance

## Maximum Acceptance Periods

| Residual Risk | Maximum Period | Minimum Review Frequency |
|---------------|---------------:|--------------------------|
| Critical | 90 Days | Monthly |
| High | 180 Days | Quarterly |
| Moderate | 12 Months | Semiannual |
| Low | 24 Months | Annual |

## Required Evidence

- Complete risk-acceptance inventory
- Updated Risk Acceptance Standard
- Reassessment records
- Compensating-control validation
- Executive approval records
- Automated workflow evidence
- Acceptance aging dashboard
- Retesting workpaper

## Success Criteria

- 100% of risk acceptances have expiration dates.
- 100% have accountable owners.
- 100% of High and Critical acceptances are reviewed on time.
- No expired acceptance remains active without reauthorization.
- Compensating controls are validated.
- Acceptance aging is reported quarterly.
- No indefinite High or Critical acceptance exists.

## Dependencies

- GRC workflow capability
- Risk-owner participation
- Governance Committee approval
- Reliable risk scoring
- Executive availability

## Remediation Risks

- Large backlog of legacy acceptances
- Disagreement regarding residual-risk ratings
- Inadequate compensating controls
- Delayed executive reauthorization
- Workflow-configuration limitations

## Closure Validation

The Quality Reviewer shall:

1. Review the revised standard.
2. Inspect all active High and Critical acceptances.
3. Select a risk-based sample of Moderate and Low acceptances.
4. Confirm expiration, review, approval, and compensating-control records.
5. Verify automated reminders and escalations.
6. Confirm at least one complete review cycle.
7. Recalculate residual risk.
8. Obtain executive closure approval.

- Ownership changes.

# Closure Requirements

A POA&M item may be closed only when:

1. All required milestones are complete.
2. Supporting evidence is retained.
3. The applicable control has been retested.
4. The original finding is no longer supported.
5. Residual risk is recalculated.
6. The risk owner approves the new residual-risk rating.
7. Quality review is complete.
8. Executive approval is obtained where required.
9. The Findings, Risk, Recommendations, and POA&M registers are updated.
10. Closure evidence is archived.

Management implementation alone does not constitute closure.
