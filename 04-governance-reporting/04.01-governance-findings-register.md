# Purpose

This Governance Findings Register consolidates all reportable governance findings identified during the cybersecurity governance assessment.

The register provides executive leadership with a centralized view of governance weaknesses, associated business risks, severity ratings, recommended corrective actions, responsible owners, and remediation status.

This document serves as the authoritative source for governance findings and supports executive reporting, remediation planning, audit readiness, and continuous improvement.

# Detailed Findings

# FIN-GOV-001

## Title

Business-Unit Cybersecurity Responsibilities Are Not Consistently Documented

### Severity

Moderate

### Business Impact

Incomplete documentation of business-unit security responsibilities may reduce accountability, complicate audits, delay incident response, and create uncertainty during personnel transitions.

### Root Cause

Business-unit role descriptions have not been updated to reflect evolving cybersecurity responsibilities.

### Recommendation

Standardize cybersecurity responsibilities across all business-unit job descriptions and validate ownership annually.

# FIN-GOV-002

## Title

Executive Cybersecurity Reporting Lacks Strategic Risk Metrics

### Severity

Moderate

### Business Impact

Executive leadership receives reliable operational metrics but limited enterprise risk information for long-term governance and investment decisions.

### Root Cause

Executive reporting evolved from operational IT reporting rather than Board-level governance reporting.

### Recommendation

Implement enterprise KRIs, residual risk reporting, cybersecurity maturity metrics, and strategic trend analysis.

### Workpaper

WP-GOV-006

### Risk Reference

RSK-GOV-002

# FIN-GOV-003

## Title

Risk Acceptances Are Not Consistently Time-Bound or Periodically Reauthorized

### Severity

High

### Business Impact

Accepted cybersecurity risks may remain active beyond approved tolerance levels without reassessment, increasing long-term organizational exposure.

### Root Cause

Risk acceptance procedures do not consistently require expiration dates or periodic reauthorization.

### Recommendation

Implement mandatory expiration dates, automated reminders, periodic reviews, and executive reauthorization requirements.

### Workpaper

WP-GOV-007

### Risk Reference

RSK-GOV-003

# FIN-GOV-004

## Title

Governance Committee Action Items Are Not Consistently Validated Before Closure

### Severity

Moderate

### Business Impact

Weak documentation of action-item completion reduces accountability and limits audit traceability.

### Root Cause

Committee administrators rely primarily on verbal status updates without retaining supporting evidence.

### Recommendation

Require documented completion evidence and formal validation before committee action items are closed.

WP-GOV-009

### Risk Reference

RSK-GOV-004

# FIN-GOV-005

## Title

Security Exceptions Are Not Consistently Reassessed Before Review Deadlines

### Severity

Moderate

### Business Impact

Outdated security exceptions may expose the organization to unnecessary residual risk and weaken governance over policy deviations.

### Root Cause

Exception tracking relies on manual reminders and inconsistent reassessment practices.

### Recommendation

Implement automated workflows for exception reviews, escalation, and executive reporting.

### Workpaper

WP-GOV-010

### Risk Reference

RSK-GOV-005

# FIN-GOV-006

## Title

Regulatory Change Impact Assessments Are Not Consistently Documented

### Severity

Low

### Business Impact

Limited documentation of regulatory impact analysis may reduce audit defensibility and consistency in implementing compliance changes.

### Root Cause

The organization monitors regulatory changes but lacks a standardized impact assessment process.

### Recommendation

Implement a standardized Regulatory Change Assessment template with documented approvals and implementation tracking.

### Workpaper

WP-GOV-011

### Risk Reference

RSK-GOV-006

# FIN-GOV-007

## Title

Board Cybersecurity Reporting Does Not Provide Sufficient Strategic Risk Information

### Severity

Moderate

### Business Impact

The Board may not receive sufficient information to evaluate enterprise cyber risk, cybersecurity maturity, or strategic investment priorities.

### Root Cause

Board reporting emphasizes operational security metrics rather than strategic governance information.

### Recommendation

Expand Board reporting to include KRIs, risk appetite, residual risk trends, cybersecurity maturity, third-party risk, and industry benchmarking.

WP-GOV-014
