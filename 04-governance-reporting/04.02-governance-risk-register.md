# Risk Scoring Methodology

Risk ratings follow:

**02.09 — Risk Scoring Methodology**

The assessment uses a five-point likelihood scale and a five-point impact scale.

```text
Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact
```

---

# Risk Rating Thresholds

| Score | Rating | Required Response |
|------:|--------|-------------------|
| 1–5 | Low | Monitor through routine governance |
| 6–10 | Moderate | Develop and track planned remediation |
| 11–15 | High | Prioritize management action and executive oversight |
| 16–25 | Critical | Immediate executive action and escalation |

---

# Likelihood Scale

| Score | Rating | Description |
|------:|--------|-------------|
| 1 | Rare | Event is highly unlikely |
| 2 | Unlikely | Event could occur but is not expected |
| 3 | Possible | Event may occur under current conditions |
| 4 | Likely | Event is expected to occur periodically |
| 5 | Almost Certain | Event is occurring or expected frequently |

# RSK-GOV-001 — Inconsistent Business-Unit Cybersecurity Accountability

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Related Finding | FIN-GOV-001 |
| Related Control | CTRL-GOV-004 |
| Related Workpaper | WP-GOV-004 |

| Status | Open |

## Risk Statement

Because cybersecurity responsibilities for selected business-unit application owners are not consistently documented, there is a possibility that ownership and accountability will become unclear during personnel changes, incidents, audits, or remediation activities, which could result in delayed security decisions and inconsistent protection of systems processing ePHI.

## Threat Sources

- Organizational change
- Personnel turnover
- Control ownership disputes
- Incomplete audit preparation
- Delayed incident coordination

## Vulnerability

Formal role descriptions and responsibility records do not consistently include cybersecurity responsibilities for all business-unit system owners.

## Affected Assets and Processes

- Business-owned applications
- Clinical systems
- Departmental technology
- Risk ownership
- Incident escalation
- Corrective-action management

## Inherent Risk

| Factor | Score |
|--------|------:|
| Likelihood | 3 — Possible |
| Impact | 4 — Major |
| Score | 12 — High |

## Existing Controls

- Enterprise organizational chart
- Security Roles and Responsibilities Matrix
- Governance Committee oversight
- Assigned technical owners
- Informal operational ownership
- Executive escalation procedures

## Residual Risk

| Factor | Score |
|--------|------:|
| Likelihood | 2 — Unlikely |
| Impact | 4 — Major |
| Score | 8 — Moderate |

## Treatment Strategy

- Update role descriptions.
- Standardize business-owner responsibilities.
- Require annual ownership certification.
- Integrate ownership validation into governance reviews.
- Maintain alternate owners for critical systems.

## Target Completion

**Q4 FY2027**

# RSK-GOV-003 — Open-Ended Cybersecurity Risk Acceptance

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Related Finding | FIN-GOV-003 |
| Related Control | CTRL-GOV-007 |
| Related Workpaper | WP-GOV-007 |
| Recommendation | REC-GOV-003 |
| Planned POA&M | POAM-GOV-003 |
| Risk Owner | Enterprise Risk Manager |
| Treatment | Mitigate |
| Status | Open |
| Executive Escalation | Required |

## Risk Statement

Because cybersecurity risk acceptances are not consistently time-bound or periodically reauthorized, there is a possibility that accepted risks will remain active after technologies, threats, business conditions, or compensating controls change, which could result in prolonged exposure of critical systems and ePHI beyond approved risk tolerance.

## Threat Sources

- Evolving cyber threats
- Control degradation
- Technology changes
- Organizational changes
- Indefinite remediation deferral
- Compensating-control failure

## Vulnerability

Selected risk acceptances lack:

- Expiration dates
- Mandatory reassessment dates
- Formal reauthorization
- Automated reminders
- Aging escalation

## Affected Assets and Processes

- Critical clinical systems
- Enterprise risk governance
- Security exception management
- Risk-treatment planning
- Executive oversight
- HIPAA risk management

## Inherent Risk

| Factor | Score |
|--------|------:|
| Likelihood | 4 — Likely |
| Impact | 5 — Severe |
| Score | 20 — Critical |

## Existing Controls

- Enterprise Risk Register
- Executive approval requirements
- Risk Committee review
- Documented compensating controls
- Quarterly High-risk reporting
- Formal risk owners

## Residual Risk

| Factor | Score |
|--------|------:|
| Likelihood | 3 — Possible |
| Impact | 4 — Major |
| Score | 12 — High |

## Treatment Strategy

- Require expiration dates for all acceptances.
- Establish risk-based maximum approval periods.
- Require quarterly High and Critical risk review.
- Require reauthorization before extension.
- Validate compensating controls.
- Implement automated expiration reminders.
- Escalate expired acceptances.
- Prohibit indefinite High or Critical risk acceptance.

## Target Completion

**Q1 FY2027**

## Interim Actions

- Review all existing accepted risks within 60 days.
- Escalate expired High and Critical acceptances.
- Assign temporary review dates.
- Confirm compensating-control effectiveness.
- Report results to the Executive Risk Committee.

# Risk Monitoring Requirements

Governance risks shall be reviewed:

- Monthly by the Governance Committee

- Before risk closure
- During the next independent assessment

# Risk Closure Requirements

A Governance risk may be closed only when:

- Required corrective actions are complete.
- Supporting evidence is available.
- The applicable control has been retested.
- The original finding has been addressed.
- Residual risk has been recalculated.
- The risk owner approves closure.
- Quality review is complete.
- Executive approval is obtained where required.
