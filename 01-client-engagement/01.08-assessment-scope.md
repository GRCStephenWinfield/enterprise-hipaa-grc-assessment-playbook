# Purpose

This document defines the official scope of the Enterprise HIPAA Cybersecurity Assessment.

The Assessment Scope establishes the organizational boundaries, business processes, facilities, technologies, information assets, and security domains that will be evaluated during the engagement.

Clearly defining scope ensures that stakeholders share a common understanding of what is included, what is excluded, and where assessment activities will occur.

# Scope Statement

The Enterprise HIPAA Cybersecurity Assessment will evaluate the effectiveness of cybersecurity governance, administrative safeguards, physical safeguards, technical safeguards, operational resilience, and regulatory compliance across Magnolia Regional Medical Center.

The assessment is designed to determine whether cybersecurity controls adequately protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of electronic protected health information (ePHI) while supporting safe and reliable healthcare operations.

# Assessment Boundaries

The assessment will evaluate:

- Control design
- Selected operating effectiveness
- Policies
- Standards
- Procedures
- Evidence
- Governance
- Risk management
- Documentation
- Management oversight

The assessment is **not** intended to certify compliance or provide legal opinions.

---

# Scope Limitations

The following limitations apply:

- No destructive testing
- No penetration testing
- No red team exercises
- No exploitation of production systems
- No modification of production configurations
- No collection of unnecessary PHI
- Limited review of third-party environments unless authorized

# Consultant Notes

## Best Practices

- Write the scope so there is no ambiguity about what is included.
- Validate the scope with business, clinical, and technical stakeholders.
- Document exclusions separately rather than implying them.

## Common Pitfalls

- Defining scope too broadly for available resources.
- Failing to identify business process boundaries.
- Ignoring cloud-hosted or vendor-managed systems.

## Red Flags

- Frequent requests to expand scope without change control.
- Critical systems omitted from the assessment.
- Stakeholders disagree on what is in scope.
- Scope changes are communicated verbally rather than documented.
