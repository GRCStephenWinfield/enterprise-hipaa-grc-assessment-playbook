# Engagement Overview

The assessment team will perform an enterprise cybersecurity and HIPAA Security Rule assessment of Magnolia Regional Medical Center.

The engagement will evaluate cybersecurity governance, risk management, regulatory compliance, administrative safeguards, physical safeguards, technical safeguards, operational resilience, and selected enterprise security capabilities.

The assessment will use an evidence-based and risk-focused methodology aligned primarily with the HIPAA Security Rule and supported by recognized cybersecurity standards and frameworks.

---

# Engagement Objectives

The assessment team will:

1. Evaluate the organization's cybersecurity governance structure.
2. Assess compliance with applicable HIPAA Security Rule requirements.
3. Review selected administrative, physical, and technical safeguards.
4. Evaluate the design and operating effectiveness of selected security controls.
5. Identify cybersecurity and compliance gaps.
6. assess risks affecting patient safety, ePHI, clinical operations, and business continuity.
7. evaluate cybersecurity maturity across the defined assessment domains.
8. develop prioritized remediation recommendations.
9. prepare executive and Board-ready reporting.
10. provide a measurable roadmap for improving cybersecurity maturity.

# Assessment Team Responsibilities

The assessment team will:

- Perform the engagement in accordance with the approved methodology.
- Protect confidential and regulated information.
- Maintain professional independence and objectivity.
- Document evidence and conclusions.
- Escalate material risks promptly.
- Avoid unauthorized or disruptive testing.
- Maintain work-product quality.
- Provide regular status reporting.
- Deliver agreed-upon assessment artifacts.

---

# Evidence Handling

Evidence shall be:

- Requested through approved channels.
- Stored in authorized repositories.
- Access-restricted.
- Protected according to data classification.
- Used only for approved assessment purposes.
- Retained and destroyed according to the engagement agreement.

The assessment team shall avoid collecting live PHI or ePHI unless specifically authorized and necessary.

Where possible, evidence should be redacted, sampled, or generated from non-production sources.

---

# Testing Restrictions

The following activities are prohibited unless separately authorized in writing:

- Destructive security testing
- Unapproved penetration testing
- Denial-of-service testing
- Social engineering against workforce members
- Exploitation of production vulnerabilities
- Modification of production configurations
- Access to live patient records beyond approved necessity
- Testing that could disrupt clinical operations
- Installation of unauthorized tools or agents

---

# Consultant Notes

## Best Practices

- Separate the strategic charter from the detailed SOW.
- Define exclusions as clearly as included services.
- Identify restricted testing activities before fieldwork.
- Tie deliverables to formal acceptance criteria.
- Require written approval for material scope changes.

## Common Pitfalls

- Using vague phrases such as “assess cybersecurity” without defining domains.
- Failing to identify client responsibilities.
- Omitting evidence-handling rules.
- Assuming technical testing is authorized.
- Leaving acceptance criteria undefined.

## Red Flags

- The client expects a guarantee of compliance.
- Scope is broader than available time or resources.
- Production testing expectations are unclear.
- Stakeholders have not agreed to participate.
- Commercial and legal responsibilities remain unresolved.
- The client requests concealment of material findings.
